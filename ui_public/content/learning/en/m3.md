# M3 – High-pressure compressor (HPC)

**What does it do?** It compresses the core air in nine stages; most of the engine's total pressure rise happens here.
It is driven by the high-pressure turbine and turns fast.

**Why is it needed?** The higher the pressure before combustion, the more efficient the cycle. The HPC is one of the
components with the strongest effect on fuel burn.

## Main parts
| Part | Function | Typical material | Manufacturing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inlet duct (S-duct) | Brings the air down from the booster radius to the HPC radius | Titanium alloy | Machining |
| Inlet guide vane (IGV) and variable stators | Adjust the flow angle at different speeds | Titanium / steel | Forging + machining |
| Rotor blades (9 rows) | Add work to the air | Titanium at the front, nickel alloy at the rear | Forging + precision machining |
| Stator vanes | Slow the flow and turn it into pressure | Titanium / nickel alloy | Forging, machining |
| Disks and spools | Hold the blades; high centrifugal load | Titanium, nickel superalloy in rear stages | Forging, friction welding |
| Case | Outer flow wall, bleed ports | Nickel alloy / steel | Ring forging + machining |
| CDP seal | Seals the compressor discharge pressure | Nickel superalloy | Forging + machining |

## Why is it critical?
- **Stall and surge:** if the flow separates the engine can surge; variable stators and bleed valves prevent it.
- **Rear stages are hot:** compression heats the air, so the last stages need nickel alloy instead of titanium.
- **Tip clearance:** the small gap between blade tip and case directly affects efficiency.
- **Life-limited parts (LLP):** disks and spools must be replaced after a set number of flight cycles.
