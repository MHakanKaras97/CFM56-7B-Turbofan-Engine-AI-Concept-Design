# M4 – Combustor

**What does it do?** It sprays fuel into the high-pressure air from the compressor and burns it continuously. The gas
temperature rises close to the melting point of the turbine materials.

**Why is it needed?** It is the only place where energy is taken from the fuel to drive the turbines and make thrust.

## Main parts
| Part | Function | Typical material | Manufacturing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diffuser | Slows the compressor air so the flame stays lit | Nickel alloy | Casting + machining |
| Outer and inner case | Form the pressure vessel around the combustor | Nickel superalloy | Forging + machining |
| Liner (flame tube) | Holds the flame; its holes admit cooling and mixing air | Nickel/cobalt sheet + thermal barrier coating | Sheet forming, laser drilling |
| Dome and swirlers | Swirl the air, mix it with fuel, anchor the flame | Cobalt alloy | Sheet, casting |
| Fuel injectors (20) | Atomise the fuel into fine droplets | Nickel alloy | Precision machining, brazing |

## Why is it critical?
- **Temperature pattern:** the gas going to the turbine must have no hot spots, or the turbine blades fail early.
- **Emissions:** the combustor design sets NOx, CO and smoke; certification limits apply.
- **Cooling:** the liner walls must stay much cooler than the flame; cooling holes and coatings do this.
