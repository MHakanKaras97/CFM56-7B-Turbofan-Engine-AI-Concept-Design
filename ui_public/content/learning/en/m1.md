# M1 – Inlet and fan

**What does it do?** It takes the air smoothly into the engine and accelerates it with the large fan. Most of the fan
air goes through the bypass and produces the largest part of the engine thrust (around three quarters).

**Why is it needed?** A turbofan's efficiency comes from accelerating a lot of air a little; the fan does exactly that.
It also gives the air going to the core its first pressure rise.

## Main parts
| Part | Function | Typical material | Manufacturing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan blade | Accelerates and compresses the air | Titanium alloy | Forging + machining |
| Fan disk | Holds the blades, transmits power to the shaft | Titanium alloy | Forging + machining |
| Spinner (nose cone) | Guides the air smoothly from the hub | Aluminium / composite | Forming |
| Fan case | Surrounds the fan, sets the tip clearance | Aluminium / composite | Machining, composite layup |
| Containment ring | Keeps a released blade inside the engine | Kevlar wrap / metal | Winding, machining |
| Outlet guide vanes (OGV) | Remove the swirl behind the fan | Aluminium / composite | Casting, composite |
| Fan frame | Main front structure; carries bearings and the aircraft mount | Titanium / steel | Casting + welding |

## Why is it critical?
- **Bird strike and blade-off:** even if a fan blade breaks, no debris may leave the case. This is a certification requirement.
- **Tip speed:** the fan blade tip is supersonic; shock losses and noise are central to the design.
- **Noise:** most of the engine's external noise comes from the fan.
