# M7 – Nozzles and turbine rear frame

**What does it do?** It accelerates the bypass air and the core gas out of the engine; this is where thrust is made.
The turbine rear frame is the engine's rear structure.

**Why is it needed?** The nozzle exit areas set the operating point of the fan and the turbines. A wrong area means lost
efficiency or compressor stall.

## Main parts
| Part | Function | Typical material | Manufacturing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bypass duct (inner and outer wall) | Carries the fan air to the nozzle | Carbon-fibre composite | Composite layup |
| Fan nozzle | Accelerates the bypass air | Aluminium / composite | Machining, layup |
| Turbine rear frame (hub, case, struts) | Carries the rear bearing; removes the LPT exit swirl | Nickel superalloy | Casting + welding |
| Core nozzle sleeve | Accelerates the core gas | Nickel alloy sheet | Sheet forming |
| Exhaust plug | Inner wall of the core nozzle, closes the flow smoothly | Nickel/titanium sheet | Sheet forming |

## Why is it critical?
- **Area = operating point:** if the nozzle areas drift from the ICD values, the balance of the whole engine changes.
- **Noise:** jet noise depends on exit velocity; the balance of fan and core jets matters.
- **Thrust reverser:** the real engine has a reverser in the fan duct (out of scope in this project).
