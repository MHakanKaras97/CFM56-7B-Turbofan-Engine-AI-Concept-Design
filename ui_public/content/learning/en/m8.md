# M8 – Shafts and bearings

**What does it do?** It carries the turbine power to the fan and the compressors, holds every rotating part on bearings
and passes the loads to the engine frames.

**Why is it needed?** Two concentric shafts turn at different speeds. Without a well-placed, vibration-free and
lubricated bearing layout the engine cannot run.

## Main parts
| Part | Function | Typical material | Manufacturing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan shaft | Transmits the LP shaft power to the fan disk | High-strength steel | Forging + machining |
| LP shaft | Long slender shaft from the LPT to the fan | Maraging steel | Forging, deep boring |
| No. 1 ball bearing | Carries the fan thrust | Bearing steel (M50) | Precision grinding |
| No. 2 roller bearing | Supports the fan shaft radially | Bearing steel | Precision grinding |
| No. 3 ball bearing | Carries the HP spool thrust | Bearing steel | Precision grinding |
| No. 4 intershaft bearing | Supports the HP spool on the LP shaft | Bearing steel | Precision grinding |
| No. 5 roller bearing | Supports the aft end of the LPT | Bearing steel | Precision grinding |
| Bearing supports (sumps) | Tie the bearings to the frames, keep the oil inside | Titanium / nickel alloy | Forging + machining |

## Why is it critical?
- **Torsion:** the LP shaft is long and thin but carries all the fan power; torsional strength is a key design limit.
- **Critical speeds:** shafts can vibrate at certain speeds; operating speeds must stay away from them.
- **Lubrication:** a bearing failure can stop the engine quickly; oil pressure and temperature are monitored all the time.
